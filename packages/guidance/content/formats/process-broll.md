# Physical processes and B-roll

Use this for assembly, unboxing, preparation, product handling, demonstrations and other changes to real objects. It is not tied to a product category.

Write the process as observable state transitions. Each shot should make one main operation readable: approach, contact/manipulation, completed change, stable result. Split complex simultaneous transformations when the viewer cannot track them. Do not add intermediate states that contradict product facts.

Track persistent objects by stable names. Record what is present, absent, open, closed, attached, detached, covered or hidden at the beginning and end. Hidden is not removed. A new object must enter visibly or be explained by an intentional time jump. `production_plan_validate` checks declared cross-shot states; the host still judges physical correctness and whether additions repeat.

Maintain counts and relationships as well as labels: what supports or contains each object, which hand/tool carries it, which parts are already applied, and what remains to be done. Release a load only after it is supported. An object outside the crop still exists; an occluded state may be unknown. A completed operation must not silently reset at the next cut. A deliberate time jump names the omitted step and starts from its resulting state. The production-quality playbook (`rikoko_guide {topic:"production-quality"}`) gives the general planning and review method.

A finished product photo is an appearance reference, not evidence of the current process stage. Where an operation depends on an intermediate arrangement, prepare and inspect that actual start image or a suitable settled frame from the previous accepted clip. Keep the boundary image's job separate from the video transition; verify object counts, support and completion states in both.

Budget time for approach, contact/manipulation, completion and a readable settled result. Specify count or dose and a stopping condition where relevant. One short clip should not independently unwrap, assemble, fill and display a product. Split operations or omit a step with an explicit edit; a provider's duration does not determine how many actions are feasible. For a pour, for example, the vessel and target stay supported, the quantity is bounded, the flow stops, and the container withdraws after the stream has ended.

For a stepwise demonstration, prompt for clear separation of the preparation, operation and result. Keep the contact area visible; avoid decorative camera moves that conceal completion. Use a motion excerpt only when it contains the same intended type of action; camera-only transfer must exclude its original subject action.

Only for a user-requested optional video review, inspect first/middle/last frames and additional samples around contact and completion. If a cut drops more than a second, inspect that tail. If the action finishes there, extend the shot or speed the whole accepted take moderately. A short reference shot is not permission to remove the result.

Within that requested review, apply event-specific inspection to the relevant operations, including occlusion, re-entry and cross-shot boundaries. Check for additions already present, duplicate parts, scale changes, unsupported loads and an apparent completion that reverses later. Uniform overview frames alone cannot establish a process pass. Dense review of the ending cannot certify the earlier steps.

The host checks critical images and observes actual footage/final output within available modalities; technical delivery remains separate from creative judgment. Do not automatically regenerate after a quality judgment. See delivery policy (`rikoko_guide {topic:"visual-review"}`).
