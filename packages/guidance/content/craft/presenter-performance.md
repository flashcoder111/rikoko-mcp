# Presenter performance and speech-led editing

Use for direct address, explanation and spoken ad passages. The format supplies the argument; this page turns it into a performance that can be seen and heard. Model-specific speech syntax belongs in `rikoko_guide {topic:"models/minimax-h3"}`.

## Direct a thought, then its expression

Descript's practical guide treats clear sound, an approachable on-camera presence and purposeful pacing as foundations of talking-head video. Supporting footage and graphics can illustrate what is being said. These are production/editorial techniques, not evidence that a generation model will deliver them. [Descript, Brandon Copple, updated July 29, 2026](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/tips-for-making-a-talking-head-video-that-people-want-to-watch).

Use a short performance card for each thought:

| Choice | Record something observable |
| --- | --- |
| Addressee | Directly into the lens for direct address; one established off-camera listener for an interview |
| Intention | Explain a step, acknowledge a frustration, reveal a useful detail, invite a next action |
| Change | Curious opening → demonstration → quiet confidence; avoid one exaggerated expression throughout |
| Voice | Language, intelligible pace, selected emphasis and the reason for a pause |
| Body | One motivated gesture whose timing does not obscure face, mouth or product |
| Exit | Finish the thought, release the gesture, leave a usable edit tail |

These choices are rikoko's synthesis. Translate “authentic” into behavior: a brief glance at the object when pointing to it, then a return to the listener. Translate “energetic” into an engaged opening and one emphasis; it need not mean shouting, constant head movement or rapid cuts. A style label alone leaves the performance undecided.

## Authored example: explain one visible control

Assume the reference product has the shown side button. Adopted dialogue: “按一下侧面的按钮，就能打开。” Performance: the presenter meets the lens, glances down while indicating the side button, makes the press, then looks back after the opening completes. The explanatory words stay calm; the result carries the emphasis. Use only if that operation is supported by the product evidence.

Give exact adopted words to `talking-head-v1.dialogue`; do not rewrite them in several action fields. Let the actual spoken take determine the edit. If the line, gesture and operation exceed the supported clip duration, split at a thought boundary or put the operation under continuing approved speech. Do not shorten required claims by silently dropping words. Voice adjectives guide delivery; they do not establish a reusable voice identity.

## Protect meaning while editing

Choose the speech passage first, then place the illustrating shot where its claim becomes relevant. Keep the corresponding audio continuous only when the edit actually carries that same take. At the return to the presenter, preserve gesture direction, object state and gaze. A cutaway can cover an edit; it cannot prove a missing operation or repair an incorrect spoken line.

Caption emphasis should follow the actual words and their emphasis. Reserve layout space around the mouth, the relevant hand and the object. Keep breaths and pauses that make the thought intelligible; remove dead time with an editorial reason. Read `rikoko_guide {topic:"craft/media-operations"}` for actual audio/caption operations rather than inventing timing.

Observe words, mouth movement, vocal delivery and edits together when playback/listening is available. Frame observations cover framing and visible identity only. If a product move succeeds but the wording fails, record those as different findings. Do not replace an explicitly requested visible speaking performance with silent footage and unrelated narration.
