# Product staging, material behavior and visual proof

Use before writing a product action, adapting a reference ad or designing a product image. The question is which visible event makes this particular product understandable or desirable.

## Make the starting image support the event

Runway's Gen-4 guide distinguishes the image's visual starting information from text describing motion. It also shows how a reference that already implies motion can work against a requested static result. This is a useful planning lesson; its model-specific prompting advice is not a verified Seedance or MiniMax rule. [Runway Gen-4 guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide).

Inspect the actual starting frame: is the hinge visible, can the hand reach the handle, is there space to lift the lid, and is the state still before the required action? A closed-state instruction conflicts with an already open image. Establish a compatible anchor through the available image workflow before expecting motion prose to repair it.

Choose a camera that answers the product question. An oblique view can reveal depth and the contact point; a frontal view can show labels; a detail can show a control. Decide whether the camera moves or the product rotates. If both move, explain their relationship so the viewer can still track the feature. Lighting direction and reflection should make the relevant surface legible rather than conceal it behind generic sparkle.

## Rebuild effects when the product changes

Separate the source effect's *communication purpose* from its *physical mechanism*. This is an authored adaptation method, not a prohibition on stylization.

| Source mechanism | What to ask after replacement | Possible target direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquid splash around a bottle | Is the new object still a liquid or associated with water? Was the splash communicating freshness, impact or texture? | A solid bar may use a firm landing, grazing light or a justified foam/contact event |
| Powder dispersing | Does this material actually shed particles? | Show surface relief or a packaging reveal when airborne particles imply a false property |
| Flexible squeeze | Does the replacement compress, bend or remain rigid? | Use the real hinge, lid or rigid rotation rather than inheriting deformation |
| Floating/exploded parts | Is this literal construction, a diagram, or a metaphor? | Preserve the expressive reveal while defining which parts separate and how they return |

Keep deliberate fantasy when it serves the brief, and make its status clear. For a solid soap bar replacing bottled gel, “freshness” might become light sweeping across the dry embossed surface; lather is appropriate only when the planned use includes water and contact. The original gel's pouring stream is no longer the same demonstration. Persist the new action and source relation decisions, not just “replace product.”

## Authored prompt pair: from adjective to event

Under-specified: “Premium earbud case, cinematic reveal, luxurious lighting.”

Directed: “The closed charging case rests on a matte plinth, its hinge on the far side. A hand steadies the base while the other lifts the lid to its stop. Both earbuds remain seated. The camera stays at a three-quarter angle so the hinge and interior remain visible. A broad side reflection reveals the curved shell; hold the open state.”

Use only if the supplied case has that mechanism. For a static hero shot, omit the opening operation and stage a slow camera reveal of the unchanged product instead. These are original examples; they carry no model success claim.

## Reference and review responsibilities

Map each asset to product identity, scene, framing, motion or style. Where a motion reference contains the old product, name the transferable motion and the properties to replace. A beauty image is not evidence of a full operation. Include prerequisite, contact, state change and stable result in the direction; put quality judgments in the plan's acceptance criteria.

Watch the required contact and transition, then inspect the result: hinge travel, material deformation, part count, label orientation, contact shadows and persistent state. Sound can reinforce the physical event, but must not invent a mechanism (for example a click on an object without a latch).
